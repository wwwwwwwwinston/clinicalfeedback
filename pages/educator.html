R2C2 Feedback Framework
Relationship, Reaction, Content, Coaching (Ramani et al., 2025)
Evidence-Based

The R2C2 model provides a structured approach to giving effective clinical feedback. Each feedback conversation should progress through these four phases:

R1
Relationship Building
Establish rapport and trust. Create a safe learning environment. Ask about the learner's experience and wellbeing before diving into feedback.
R2
Exploring Reactions
Ask the learner to self-assess first. Explore their emotional reaction. "How do you think that went?" acknowledges their perspective before sharing yours.
C1
Exploring Content
Share specific, observable performance evidence. Balance strengths with areas for development. Use concrete examples from the clinical encounter.
C2
Coaching for Change
Collaboratively develop action plans. Set specific, achievable goals. Agree on follow-up and support needed. Empower the learner to drive their development.
Quick Tips for Effective Feedback
Evidence-based strategies
Be Specific

Replace "good job" with specific observable behaviours: "Your open-ended questions helped the patient disclose their dietary concerns."

Timely Delivery

Provide feedback as close to the event as possible. Same-day feedback is most effective for behaviour change.

Ask Before Telling

Invite learner self-assessment before offering your observations. This promotes reflection and shared understanding.

Focus on Behaviour, Not Person

Frame feedback around actions and outcomes, not personality traits. "The documentation was incomplete" vs "You're disorganised."

Close the Loop

Always end with agreed actions, timelines, and a plan to check progress. Follow-up shows commitment to the learner's development.

Feedback Conversation Starters
Phrases aligned to R2C2 framework stages

Relationship & Reaction

"How do you think that consultation went?"
"What aspects are you most proud of?"
"Is there anything you'd do differently?"

Content & Coaching

"I noticed specifically that you..."
"One thing that could strengthen this is..."
"What would help you work on this?"
"Let's agree on a plan for next week..."
